Working paper (not peer reviewed), version 1.0.1, 21 October 2026. Code, data, results and the
paper (PDF) for a pre-registered test of whether symmetry breaks in the *Book of Abramelin*'s
letter squares mark copying slips, across three copies.

**What changed in 1.0.1.** Documentation update: adds a 'How AI was used' section, tidies wording and removes files not needed to check the results. Results and data unchanged.

**Abstract.** The fourth book of the *Book of Abramelin* (*Des Abraham von Worms Buch der wahren Praktik von der alten Magie*, written about 1595–1609) holds about 250 letter squares, most of them symmetric word squares of the SATOR kind. Kollatsch (2021) published the 257 squares of the best copy (Wolfenbüttel, Cod. Guelf. 47.13 Aug. 4°, W1) and used their symmetry by hand to correct its slips. We test that method on copies it was not applied to. We transcribed the squares of a second German copy, Leipzig, Universitätsbibliothek, Cod. mag. 15 (L, 250 squares), with a language-model reader followed by checks against the page images, and parsed the squares of Mathers's 1900 English translation of the French copy (M, 242). The tests, controls and corrections were written down and committed before any score was computed. By a fixed binomial rule, 237 of 257 W1 squares, 207 of 250 L squares and 115 of 242 M squares have at least one symmetry, against none of 257,000, one of 250,000 and none of 242,000 shuffled controls. Where a symmetry is broken in one copy, a held-out copy differs at the flagged cell far more often than at ordinary cells, in all four pre-registered directions after Holm's correction. In the comparison untouched by Kollatsch's editing, Leipzig against Mathers, 9 of 12 flagged cells differ against 15.8 % of ordinary cells, and Mathers has the letter the symmetry predicts 5 times where 1.0 is expected. The method is precise but sees little: only 3–19 % of the cells where two copies differ lie in broken symmetries, because most changes kept the symmetry. Slips that W1 still carries recur in L (8 shared, 0.91 expected) and in Mathers (7 shared, 0.36 expected), while L and Mathers share no more than chance (3, against 0.95). The evidence weighs toward Kollatsch's symmetry method being sound where it applies, and toward L and the French line descending from W1 or a close relative of it. A family tree of the copies needs at least one more transcribed copy.

**Results in brief**

- **Most squares are true word squares.** 237 of 257 squares in Kollatsch's W1 text, 207 of 250
  in Leipzig Cod. mag. 15 (L) and 115 of 242 in Mathers (M) have at least one symmetry by a
  fixed binomial rule; of 749,000 shuffled control squares, one passes.
- **Symmetry breaks point at copying slips.** In all four pre-registered directions a held-out
  copy differs at flagged cells far more often than at ordinary cells (Holm-corrected). Leipzig
  against Mathers, the pair untouched by Kollatsch's editing: 9 of 12 flagged cells differ,
  against 15.8 % of ordinary cells (Holm p 2.7e-5); Mathers against Leipzig: 4 of 5, against
  17.9 % (Holm p 0.009).
- **The other copy often has the letter the symmetry predicts:** L → M 5 times where 1.00 is
  expected (p 0.0007); M → L 3 against 0.61 (p 0.011).
- **But symmetry catches little:** only 3–19 % of the cells where two copies differ inside
  symmetric squares lie in broken symmetries; most changes kept the symmetry.
- **Shared slips:** W1's surviving slips recur in L (8 shared, 0.91 expected; Holm p 0.0003) and
  in Mathers (7, 0.36 expected; Holm p 0.0003); L and Mathers share no more than chance (3, 0.95
  expected; p 0.065). The evidence weighs toward L and the French line descending from W1 or a
  close relative of it.
- **Robust to letter folding:** the same conclusions without i/j and u/v folding (pre-registered)
  and with I and T folded (post hoc).
- **Observation, not tested:** L has 37 squares that keep only the half-turn symmetry, W1 has 3.

**Limits.** W1 is Kollatsch's edited text, assembled from letter-group sequences, and its images
were not seen, so the clean evidence is Leipzig against Mathers. The Leipzig transcription was
made by a language-model reader (Claude API) and checked against the page images by Claude;
it awaits a palaeographer's reading. The corrections made the result weaker, not stronger. Mathers's
squares are a 1999 typesetting of a printed translation, many partly empty. With three copies
the shared-slip test ranks pairs; it is not a family tree. The pre-registration was committed in
the author's private working repository (commit eb1d554), so readers cannot check its timing. The
I/T-folded run is post hoc.
